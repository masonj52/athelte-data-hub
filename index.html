    How each number is made

    Every figure here is measured against your own history, never against other people.

    Readiness

    a score from 0 to 100

    Three readings are turned into distances from your own 60 day baseline and blended: HRV counts for 45 percent, resting heart rate for 30 percent with higher counted as worse, and last night’s sleep against a 7.5 hour need for 25 percent.

    Green is 70 or more, Amber is 50 to 69, Red is under 50. A night shift today or yesterday, or a main sleep whose middle sits 3 hours or more from your usual, holds the day at Amber.

    Heart rate variability

    nightly RMSSD in ms

    The Fitbit reports one value per night: the variation between heartbeats while you sleep. Higher than your usual tends to mean better recovered. The app compares each night with your baseline and also watches the 7 day average, because single nights swing with late meals, alcohol and short sleep.

    Reading the trend against a personal baseline follows Plews and colleagues, 2013. The measure itself is defined in the 1996 Task Force standards.

    Resting heart rate

    beats per minute

    The day’s resting rate against your 60 day average. A rate that sits several beats above your usual for days in a row is a reason to ease off; one high morning is not.

    Sleep and sleep debt

    hours, by stage

    Stage segments less than an hour apart are joined into one sleep and counted on the day you woke. The longest sleep is the main one; the rest are naps, and both count toward the total.

    Debt adds up the hours you fell short of 7.5 across the last seven nights. Nights over the need do not cancel it.

    Session load

    minutes times effort

    Sessions you log here are written to the Log tab of your sheet. Each one scores its minutes multiplied by how hard it felt from 1 to 10. One number then covers lifting, running and soccer alike.

    The method comes from Foster and colleagues, 2001, who checked it against heart rate during steady work, intervals and basketball.

    Acute to chronic ratio

    this week against this month

    A weighted 7 day load divided by a weighted 28 day load, shown once you have logged for 28 days. Above about 1.5 means this week is much heavier than you are used to.

    Treat it as a trend, not a forecast. Impellizzeri and colleagues, 2020 found no evidence that steering training by this ratio lowers injury risk.

    Monotony

    how alike your days are

    The average daily load over a week divided by how much it varies. Above about 2, every day looks the same, hard days and easy days have blurred together.

    A training aid, not medical advice. Pain that lingers or a reading that worries you belongs with a clinician.
