      How the numbers work

      Deck
      Tempo

      Your readiness score from 0 to 100, shown as 90 plus half the score. A score of 60 reads 120. The colour runs red below 40, yellow in the 50s, green from 60 to 70, blue from 80 to 90 and white above 90.

      Readiness score

      Three readings are measured against your own 60 day baseline and blended: HRV 45 percent, resting heart rate 30 percent with higher counted as worse, and last night’s sleep against a 7.5 hour need 25 percent. The training call under the dial uses the sheet’s zones: 70 or more, 50 to 69, under 50. A night shift or a sleep 3 hours off your usual time holds the call at the middle zone.

      HRV

      One value per night: the variation between heartbeats while you sleep. Reading the trend against a personal baseline follows Plews and colleagues, 2013. The measure is defined in the 1996 Task Force standards.

      Sleep and debt

      Stage segments less than an hour apart are joined into one sleep and counted on the day you woke. Debt adds the hours you fell short of 7.5 across the last seven nights.

      Calories

      Burned is everything for the day, resting included, as Google Health totals it. Active is the part above resting. Workout calories are the active burn inside a recorded workout.

      Load and ratio

      Each logged session scores minutes times effort, after Foster and colleagues, 2001. The ratio divides a weighted 7 day load by a weighted 28 day load once you have 28 days logged. Treat it as a trend: Impellizzeri and colleagues, 2020 found no evidence that steering by it lowers injury risk.

      A training aid, not medical advice.
